We ran 1,000 prompt-injection attempts past a model that never writes a single token.

Jev (TypeSafe) returns a typed decision with a probability. No text. So we raced it against every current Claude model on the same labelled texts, half attacks, half harmless.

Jev → 92% correct, 0.5 s per decision, 3 cents per 1,000.

Claude Haiku 4.5 → 94% correct, 5.8 s per decision, $3.50 per 1,000 at list price.

Claude Sonnet 5 → 98% correct, 3.0 s, $5 per 1,000.

So on the guardrail task Jev lands at Haiku level, a few points under the Sonnets and Opuses, at a tenth of the time and a hundredth of the cost.

The interesting part is how they fail. Jev almost never flags a harmless message but misses about one attack in eight. Haiku catches nearly every attack but flags one harmless message in seven.

The video shows one decision as it happens, then a thousand, then the ranking.

Measured, not quoted: our own run, Claude via Claude Code at low effort with thinking off, Jev via OpenRouter, 200-case preliminary cut of a 1,000-case set, 95% intervals in the report. Full run and code on GitHub, link in comments.
