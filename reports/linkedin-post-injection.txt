We ran prompt-injection attempts past a model that never writes a single token.

Jev (TypeSafe) returns a typed decision with a probability. No text, no reasoning, no streaming.

So we raced it against every current Claude model on the same labelled messages: half attacks, half harmless.

Jev → 90% correct, 0.5 s per decision, 3 cents per 1,000.

Claude Haiku 4.5 → 92% correct, 5.8 s, $3.55 per 1,000.

Claude Sonnet 5 → 98% correct, 3.0 s, $5.15 per 1,000.

So Jev detects attacks on par with Haiku, twelve times faster and a hundred times cheaper. Sonnet and Opus are several points more accurate, and several seconds slower.

The interesting part is how they fail.

Jev almost never flags a harmless message, but misses roughly one attack in eight.

Haiku catches nearly every attack, and flags one harmless message in seven.

For a guardrail on every chat turn, that trade-off matters more than the headline number.
